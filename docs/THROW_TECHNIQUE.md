# Throw animation references

The sprites are original pixel art. Athlete references guide the mechanics; proportions, poses and timing are adapted for the game's big-headed characters. No photographs, footage, athlete likenesses or branded kit are included as game assets.

## Reference cues by technique

| Event and game technique | Athlete reference | Movement carried into the animation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shot: standing put | The delivery portion of David Storl's glide | Sink slowly into the power position, deep over the right leg with the free arm wrapped across, then drive through the hips, keep the shot at the jaw and strike late, finishing tall on the toes. The beginner omits the glide. |
| Shot: glide / power glide | David Storl | Start tall on the right leg, fold down with the left knee tucked in, kick the left leg low toward the board while the right drives and pulls under, land low on the right foot with closed shoulders, plant the left and rise into the put. Power glide adds a reverse after release. |
| Shot: rotational | Ryan Crouser | A slow wind with the shoulders turned right, then ever faster: a wide, low sweep of the right leg round the bent left, a short low flight, a closed right-foot catch, the left foot down almost at once, and a late strike. This is a compact conventional rotation, not a recreation of Crouser's individual slide variant. |
| Discus: standing / half turn | Standing delivery and the South African / sprint-turn drill | Standing throws omit travel. The half-spin starts at the rear facing the sector on the left support leg, right foot behind. Step through, drive the right knee into a low flight to the middle, land on the right toe, turn and plant the left, then let the hips lead the long-arm strike. It omits the full throw's rear-facing wind and left-foot entry. |
| Discus: full turn / fast spin | Sandra Perković (now Sandra Elkasević), Mykolas Alekna, Daniel Ståhl | Pre-swings on soft knees, ending on a backswing wound right round so the entry starts from the discus's furthest point back. A slow, wide entry on the bent left leg (weight over, right leg sweeping, right knee swinging through), a low quick drive with only a brief flight, a right-foot pivot and the left foot down straight after. The hips open, then the chest, and the long arm strikes last at shoulder height. After the reverse the turn runs on round to the back of the circle. |
| Hammer: winds and turns | Yuriy Sedykh, Paweł Fajdek | The head starts on the ground behind the right foot and the first wind sweeps it up. Two winds, then turns that are about half double support (hips low as the hammer drops through its low point at the back of the circle) and half single support on the left foot (hips higher, the hammer over its high point toward the sector). Most of the travel across the circle happens in single support. The wire is as long as the cage allows and the orbit steepens until the low point skims the ground. Release comes on the rise at about head height, an eighth of a turn before the high point, extending the legs and back while leaning away from the hammer. Recovery turns use empty, relaxed hands. |
| Javelin: three-step / full approach | Coaching three-step progression, full approach mechanics and Neeraj Chopra's delivery | The short form is already withdrawn and takes left-right-left into the block. The full form starts farther back carrying overhead, builds speed, withdraws on a left-foot contact and travels through successive crossovers before the right-foot landing and left block. Both finish with hip drive, elbow-led delivery, downward arm sweep and right-leg catch. |
| Javelin: crossover / elite approach | Neeraj Chopra | Add a longer, low impulse stride before the block. The body leans well back over the right leg while the lower body leads; the release comes with the chest already past the braced left leg. Block to release is the quickest part of the throw. |

These are selected movement cues, not motion capture or measured reproductions of those athletes. Standing and half-turn variants are game teaching progressions derived from parts of a full throw. Sedykh's documented throw uses three turns; the game's existing two/three/four-turn progression is retained. Similarly, a glide is a valid elite technique; the form ladder is a game convention, not a ranking of real techniques.

## Video review: discus and javelin

The following videos were inspected as sequences of actual frames, including the foot contacts, both arms and the position of the pelvis relative to the shoulders. They supplement the photographs and coaching documents below.

- [Branko Paukovic, javelin throw](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Branko_Paukovic,_javelin_throw.webm), Sdcsabac / Diagnostic Sports Center Šabac, 2014, entire 2.13-second clip. The throwing hand trails a nearly level shaft during the crossovers; the opposite arm leads. The lower body advances ahead of the leaning-back chest. The right foot lands before the left block, the free elbow closes toward the ribs, and only then does the throwing elbow lead the strike. The chest folds over the block before the right leg catches the body.
- [Escuela Virtual de Deportes / COLDEPORTES, El giro o paso sprint](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:EVD-disco-003.ogv), 2009. The sector-facing preparation around 20–28 seconds and the repeated drive/pivot demonstrations around 155–185 seconds guide the South African drill. The left leg supports the start, the right knee drives forward, and the athlete catches low over the ball of the right foot while the left leg turns toward its plant. The long throwing arm trails; the bent free arm guides the turn, then folds toward the chest during delivery.
- [Lanzamiento de disco](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lanzamiento_de_disco.webm), ProyectoFandom / UPV/EHU collaboration, 2021. The grip close-up at the beginning and full-turn demonstration around 60–63 seconds guide the palm-over-disc grip, bent-knee entry, wide sweep, quick centre catch and turn after release. This is a coaching demonstration, not elite competition footage.

| Phase | Legs and weight | Throwing arm | Other arm |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Discus sprint start / drive | Weight on bent left support, then a low right-knee drive | Long behind the shoulder; palm above the disc and fingers over the rim | Bent and leading toward the sector |
| Discus centre / delivery | Catch low on the right toe; plant left before the hips rise and open | Stays behind the hips and chest until the late long-arm strike | Guides the turn, then folds to the torso |
| Javelin crossovers | Low passing steps alternate left and right support; final impulse briefly airborne | Withdrawn behind the shoulder, shaft close to level | Leads along the runway, visibly separated from the large head |
| Javelin block / delivery | Right foot catches, left leg braces; pelvis advances before chest | Stays long through the block, then elbow leads the overarm strike | Closes beside the ribs before the throwing arm comes through |

Explicit pelvis offsets in the delivery poses let the hips lead a still-closed chest, rather than moving the head, trunk and hips backward together. Every javelin stride has a contact pose and a toe-off pose, and the delivery adds a free-arm closing pose between the block and hip drive. The discus catches over a bent right leg, then rises onto the braced left side. The implement launch point accounts for the disc sitting below the palm.

Arm proportions match the small torso and legs: the drawn javelin arm reaches about seven sprite pixels from its shoulder, and the extended discus arm about six. Both upper arm and forearm stay compact through the release and recovery. Separate shoulder anchors show the javelin's closed chest during crossovers, then the throwing shoulder coming forward and rising as the hips drive. Discus shoulders open into the strike while the free arm folds in. The javelin is gripped on its cord at the balance point, so more shaft trails behind the hand than leads it. On the run-up it is carried level above the shoulder at forehead height, with the palm under it and the forearm up behind the ear, clear of the face; this raised carry is the one pose whose reach goes slightly past the compact arm. Withdrawn, the arm stays long behind with the tip by the brow, the shaft angled a little up along the arm. The sprite canvas is wide enough for the full shaft. Rendering and implement attachment positions use the same shoulder, elbow and hand geometry.

Discus full-turn preparation uses a dedicated planted stance for the forward swing and backswing: both shoes keep the same positions as the chest winds around them. The wide right-leg sweep starts during the subsequent left-foot entry. The right foot retains its centre catch position through the plant and hip drive. The South African drill uses the same quick finish after its step-through.

## Rhythm and timing

Contact times come from elite phase lengths, scaled to the meet's technique window (2.3 s for javelin, 2.1 s for discus) without changing meet durations.

| Javelin (crossover approach) | Time |
| --- | --- |
| Standing start, lean and first push | about 0.5 s |
| Carry strides, then the withdrawal over two strides and one crossover | 230–265 ms each, slightly quicker each stride |
| Penultimate: last left contact, then a long, low airborne impulse to the right catch | 320 ms, the longest stride in time and distance |
| Final step: right catch to left block | 160 ms |
| Block to release: free arm, hips, elbow, release | 130 ms (150 ms novice, 120 ms elite) |

The rhythm reads "da · da · da · da · daa-da-DUM". The full approach runs three carry strides with the javelin over the shoulder, withdraws on a left contact, crosses over and takes off from the left for the penultimate. The three-step form starts withdrawn, settles, then goes left, right, left. Each stride lands where `throw-motion.js` plants it: the body is placed from the supporting shoe's position in the pose, so a support foot never slides. The body travels at about the same speed through the crossovers and the impulse. It only brakes once the left leg blocks.

| Discus (one and a half turns) | Share of the turn after the deepest backswing |
| --- | --- |
| Double support: weight onto the left, the right foot still pushing | about a quarter |
| Left-foot single support: sweep and right knee through | nearly a third |
| Flight | under a tenth |
| Right-foot pivot in the middle | about a quarter |
| Left plant to release | about a sixth, the quickest phase |

The forward swing and backswing are eased and unhurried, and everything after them is slow to fast. The pelvis heading leads the chest from the entry to the strike.

Both discus arms are swung by the chest heading, rather than interpolated between drawings. The long throwing arm trails a set angle behind the right shoulder line, and the free arm leads from the left. Each hand projects to screen position and depth. An arm behind the torso is drawn behind it, so the discus is hidden behind the body as the entry faces the camera. It reappears trailing through the flight and the pivot, swings round behind on the rear side, and is slung through toward the sector at release. The hands stay below the big chin so the long arm never smears across the face. The angles are slightly shallower than real lag at the deepest points, so the discus stays readable at pixel scale.

Discus and javelin interpolate shoulders, elbows, hands, knees and shoes between contacts. Left and right limbs retain their identities when the drawing order or facing changes. The held implement follows the interpolated hand, including the javelin's changing shaft angle. Only the discus preparatory swing eases at its reversals; travel does not stop at each contact. The last technique pose joins the release, and the release joins recovery with continuous hand positions. Meet rendering, club practice, afterimages and the study page consume this same motion data.

Every javelin form includes a penultimate: a last left contact and toe take-off, a long low impulse with the right knee leading, a right-foot catch ahead of the leaning-back body, then a quicker, shorter left block step. The three-step and full forms include this sequence as well as the developed forms; elite form lengthens the split. The meet's javelin origin leaves enough runway on screen for the withdrawn approach.

Discus chest and pelvis headings follow one continuous counterclockwise turn after the preparatory wind. Those headings control the projected shoulders, hips, head facing and which limbs appear nearer the camera. The rear-facing stance projects narrowly along the sector direction, with shoe depth distinguishing the two feet. Entry loads the planted left foot while the right leg sweeps free; the centre catch gathers the free leg before the left plant. This avoids both a broad sideways straddle in the wind and inconsistent facing changes during the turn. Club practice preserves the whole animation when mirrored toward a west-facing sector.

## Recovery styles

Technique comes from form; the finish after release is the athlete's own, so two throwers at the same level still look different. `recoveryStyle` in `src/render/throw-motion.js` gives each athlete one style for every event: dancers and ex-gymnasts are Classic, class clowns, showboats and former sprinters Bouncy, chess prodigies, poker faces and marathoners Composed, and everyone else by id.

| Event | Classic | Bouncy | Composed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shot | Gliders reverse; rotational throwers let the turn run on to the back of the circle | Reverse with a hop on the right foot | No reverse: the finish held tall over the left leg |
| Discus | Spinners reverse and turn on to the back of the circle | Reverse with a hop on the right foot | No reverse: the arm wraps across over planted feet |
| Hammer | Three quarters of a turn more, then plant | A turn and a quarter, or a hop back for standing finishes | No turn: plant and sit back |
| Javelin | Fold over the block, reverse and stand | A second hop on the right foot after the reverse | Hold the fold over the block, then rise |

Fouls use the same finish for everyone, carrying over the front. The style only changes the recovery: it does not alter distance, timing, saves or form. The study page has a Recovery selector.

## Animation and review

Contact timings in `src/render/throw-motion.js` separate preparation, travel, catch, leg drive and the strike. Shot and discus no longer hold a release pose throughout their release interval: the empty arm sweeps across before the recovery settles. Hammer feet stay on the circle through planted support; the knee bend lives in the sprite pose rather than moving the whole character off the floor. Advanced hammer recovery still turns after release.

Javelin movement reserves space for the complete outlined shoe at the game's double sprite scale. A legal throw stays behind the near edge of the arc through release, recovery and the subsequent reaction; a foul still travels over it. Phase boundaries join at the same body position. Shot and javelin launch from the release hand; discus launches from the implement centre below its overhand grip; hammer launches from the final orbit's head, at the end of its wire.

Run `npm run dev` and open `/scripts/throw-study.html` for all four events at every form level, with pause, speed, time scrubbing and foul controls. The Compare selector enlarges discus half/full, javelin three-step/full or crossover/elite side by side, with the same appearance on both athletes to isolate the movement. Direct links are `/scripts/throw-study.html?focus=discus` and `/scripts/throw-study.html?focus=jav`. It uses production sprites, motion and event timings. Review at half speed, then 1×; inspect the catch, delivery and follow-through as well as the run/turn. The meet keeps its existing duration, speed controls, afterimage reduction and result holds. The animation changes do not alter distance, stats, saves or form unlock thresholds.

`tests/throw-motion.test.js` checks frame validity, approach and phase continuity, ordered contact/drive poses, support feet that never slide, javelin stride rhythm, the discus orbit, planted hammer turns, implement launch positions, follow-throughs and legal/foul javelin foot clearance.
